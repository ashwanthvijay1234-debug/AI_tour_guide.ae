02 — About the AI

Generative AI and NLP, doing the planning work.

AI_tour_guide.ae is not a database of fixed tours. It is a language model given a carefully engineered brief, and the pipeline runs in four steps.

01

Understanding preferences

Destination, duration, budget and interest tags become a natural-language brief — the same sentences a human travel agent would be told. Natural Language Processing lets the model read that brief the way a person does, including what is implied rather than stated: “family” means shorter days and fewer late nights.

02

Prompt engineering

The app wraps your choices in a system instruction fixing the model's role, the currency (AED), the pacing rules and the exact JSON shape of the answer. Constraining the output format is what turns a chatty model into a reliable component of an application.

03

Generative reasoning

Gemini predicts the plan token by token, balancing geography (grouping nearby stops), timing (heat, prayer times, opening hours) and money (spreading the budget across days). Nothing is retrieved from a list — every itinerary is generated.

04

Structured rendering

The reply is parsed back into data — days, stops, times, costs, tips — then drawn into the studio, typeset into the PDF and plotted on the map. If the model answers in prose instead of JSON, the app renders the text safely rather than failing.

03 — Problem statement

A printed guidebook cannot know who is reading it.

Traditional travel guides and static websites are written once, for an average traveller who does not exist. Every real trip breaks their assumptions.

Not personal

One plan for everyone

A guidebook's “three days in Dubai” is identical for a photographer, a shopper and a family with a toddler. It cannot weight the day toward what a specific traveller cares about.

Not flexible

Fixed time, fixed money

Most guides assume a standard week and an unstated budget. Travellers with two days, or AED 1,500 instead of 15,000, must redo the whole plan themselves.

Not current

Out of date on arrival

Opening hours, ticket prices, new attractions and seasonal closures change faster than editions are printed, so parts of every guide are wrong before it is bought.

Not efficient

Hours of manual research

Planning a UAE trip today means twenty browser tabs, three review sites and a spreadsheet — and the result is still a guess about how far apart things are.

04 — The AI solution

Instant, dynamic, hyper-personalised planning.

AI_tour_guide.ae replaces the static guide with a generative model that writes a new plan for every traveller, every time.

Personalised

Built around your inputs

Interests, budget and pace steer the generation, so a desert-and-culture trip and a shopping-and-food trip through the same city look nothing alike.

Adaptive

Any length, any budget

One day or fourteen, AED 500 or 30,000 — the model rebalances the whole itinerary rather than trimming a fixed template.

Practical

Times, costs, travel logic

Each stop carries a time window, a dirham estimate and a reason, and nearby places are grouped so the day is not spent in taxis.

Portable

Download and go

Every plan exports to a clean PDF that works offline, on paper, and on a phone with no signal in the desert.

Designed for the Emirates

Golden hour, planned by machine.

The model is briefed on UAE realities that generic travel AI misses: midday heat in summer, Friday prayer timings, modest dress at mosques, Ramadan hours, and the real drive times between Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the northern emirates.

06 — FAQ

Questions a marker usually asks.

Which AI model does this use?

Google's Gemini family, called directly from the browser over the REST API. The model is selectable in the studio — gemini-2.5-flash by default, with gemini-3-flash and gemini-2.5-pro available. Without an API key the app falls back to a built-in offline planner so the demo never fails.

Where is my API key stored?

Only in your own browser's local storage. It is never sent anywhere except directly to Google's API endpoint from your machine, and there is no server behind this project at all.

How is the PDF produced?

jsPDF builds the document in the browser from the generated data — headings, day blocks, stops, costs and tips are typeset as real text, not a screenshot, so the file stays small, selectable and printable.

Is the itinerary data real?

The places, prices and timings are real UAE landmarks and realistic 2026 estimates, but they are AI-generated suggestions and should be checked against official sites before booking.

What was built by hand here?

All of it: the interface, the prompt engineering, the JSON parsing, the PDF typesetting, the map integration and the offline fallback planner. No frameworks, no page builders — one HTML file.
