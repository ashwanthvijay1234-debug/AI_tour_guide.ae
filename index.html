A guidebook is written once for everybody. This one is written now, for you, and rewritten the moment you ask.

01 — Features

Four things the guidebook cannot do.

Generative

Written fresh, every time

No stored tours and no database of packages. Each itinerary is composed on request from your own four settings, so two travellers never receive the same document.

Conversational

Refine it by talking

“Make day 3 cheaper.” “We have a toddler.” The plan is rebuilt around plain-English notes instead of a form you have to fill in again.

Document

A live PDF, always current

Every generation typesets a real A4 document with selectable text — not a screenshot — rendered in the page before you download it.

Spatial

Every stop on the map

Each activity becomes a map button, labelled by day, so an itinerary stops being a list of names and becomes a route you can actually see.

02 — About the AI

Generative AI and NLP, doing the planning work.

The whole project is one pipeline: turn four settings into language, constrain the model hard enough to trust it, let it reason, then turn the answer back into something you can read, print and walk.

01

Understanding preferences

Your inputs become a natural-language brief. NLP reads what is implied as much as what is typed — “family” means shorter days and earlier evenings, not just a checkbox.

02

Prompt engineering

A system instruction fixes the role, the currency, the pacing rules and the exact JSON shape. Constraining the output is what turns a chatty model into a reliable component.

03

Generative reasoning

The model balances geography, timing and money token by token, writing a plan that did not exist before. Nothing is retrieved from a list of pre-made tours.

04

Structured rendering

The answer is parsed back into data, drawn on screen, typeset into PDF and plotted on the map. If the model replies in prose instead, that renders safely rather than failing.

03 — Problem statement

A printed guidebook cannot know who is reading it.

Not personal

One book, every traveller

A student on AED 2,000, a family of five and a honeymoon couple are handed the same 300 pages and left to do the filtering themselves.

Not flexible

Plans change; paper does not

A 44 °C afternoon, a closed attraction or a tired toddler rewrites the day — and nothing on the page moves to meet it.

Not current

Out of date at printing

Prices, opening hours and whole attractions change faster than editions do. In a city that opens landmarks yearly, that gap is wide.

Not efficient

Hours of manual work

Cross-referencing maps, prices and timings across seven emirates takes an evening per trip — and usually still produces too much driving.

04 — The AI solution

Instant, dynamic, hyper-personalised planning.

Personalised

Built around your four answers

Destination, days, budget and interests set the shape of every day — pacing, price band and the balance between heritage, desert and coast.

Adaptive

Rewritten on request

Notes accumulate. Ask for a lighter day 4 and the model keeps that constraint in mind for every plan it writes afterwards.

Practical

Priced, timed and grouped

Stops carry a time window and a real dirham cost, and each day is grouped geographically so you are not crossing the city twice before lunch.

Portable

A PDF you can hand over

One tap produces an A4 document with real selectable text, ready to print, forward to family or read offline on the plane.

Local knowledge

Golden hour, planned by machine.

Generic travel AI will happily send you to an outdoor souk at two in the afternoon in July. The realities the model is told about here are the ones that actually decide whether a day works: midday heat from May to September, Friday prayer timings, modest dress at mosques, Ramadan hours, and the real drive time between Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

So the desert safari lands in the late afternoon, the Grand Mosque gets the cooler slot, the souks move to the evening — and the plan reads like it was written by somebody who has been here.

06 — FAQ

Questions a marker usually asks.

Which AI model does it use?

Google Gemini, called straight from the browser. The studio defaults to gemini-3.6-flash and you can pick another from the Model & API key panel. If a model is retired or busy, the app walks a fallback chain of models it has confirmed your key can actually call, and tells you in the chat which one answered.

Where is my API key stored?

Only in your own browser's localStorage, on your own machine. There is no backend of any kind, so the key is never uploaded anywhere except directly to Google's endpoint from your computer. It is not in the repository and it must never be committed.

How is the PDF made?

With jsPDF, in the browser, on every generation. The document is laid out by hand — header band, day rules, per-stop lines, page-aware breaks and a numbered footer — so the result is real selectable text rather than a screenshot of the page. It is previewed in an iframe before you download it.

Is the data real?

The places are real UAE landmarks and the prices are realistic dirham figures, but they are indicative, not live. Nothing is fetched from a booking API, so check current prices and opening hours before you commit to a day.

What did you actually build by hand?

All of it, in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript: the layout and design system, the offline planner with its seeded shuffle, the prompt and its JSON contract, the Gemini fallback chain, the parser, the renderer, the map panel, the PDF typesetting, the typing animation and the notes window. Two CDN files are used — jsPDF and the Inter font. No framework, no build step, no server.

The lesson of the build is smaller than the feature list: generative AI is most useful when it is given structure — a clear role, real constraints and a defined output. Left open-ended it writes pleasant paragraphs. Pinned to a role, a currency, a pacing rule and an exact JSON shape, it becomes a component you can build a product on.
